01

Overview

Two imaging scales, the same animals, one coordinate space.

Parkinson's disease combines aggregation of α-synuclein with loss of dopaminergic neurons. MRI describes tissue across the whole brain; light-sheet microscopy shows molecular signal at micrometre sampling. PDBrain-LM provides both for every animal.

The dataset contains 37 mouse brains: 18 injected with α-synuclein preformed fibrils (PFF) and 19 injected with α-synuclein monomer as controls. Each animal has ex vivo 9.4 T MRI and whole-brain light-sheet microscopy (LSM) with a label-free autofluorescence channel and a tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) channel that marks dopaminergic structures; 26 animals also have a channel for α-synuclein phosphorylated at serine 129 (pS129), the marker of pathology.

MRI and LSM of each animal are registered with the PRISM pipeline into the DevCCF reference space, where Allen CCFv3 labels are available. Three learning tasks are defined on the paired data, each with a fixed evaluation protocol.

  • Disease and control groups

    Public whole-brain LSM resources mostly serve anatomical and circuit mapping, activity mapping and cell detection. Here the cohort is a disease model with matched controls, at two time points.

  • Paired across scales

    MRI and LSM come from the same brains and are registered subject by subject, so microscopic pathology can be related directly to MRI signal.

  • Built for learning

    Atlas-defined regions, animal-level data splits and baseline results are given for classification, cross-channel synthesis and guided super-resolution.

Dataset

Cohort, modalities and the grids the data are used on.

All animals come from one multimodal study of α-synucleinopathy in C57BL/6J mice (Combes, Dong et al.). Each mouse received a unilateral injection of mouse α-synuclein PFFs, or of monomer, into the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc) and was examined 12 or 20 weeks post-injection (wpi).

Anatomical annotation

The P56 DevCCF incorporates the Allen Mouse Brain Common Coordinate Framework (CCFv3), so every registered volume can be divided into standard regions of interest: cortical and subcortical regions, white-matter tracts and ventricles. The inverse transforms carry the same labels back into each animal's own space.

03

Acquisition

From injection to registered volumes. Animal work and imaging follow the source study.

  1. Injection

    Mouse α-synuclein PFFs, or monomer as control, are injected into the SNc of one hemisphere.

  2. Incubation

    Animals are examined 12 or 20 weeks after injection.

  3. Ex vivo MRI

    Fixed heads are scanned at 9.4 T with the brain left in the skull: T1-weighted, DTI and SWI, with QSM reconstructed from the SWI data.

  4. Clearing and labelling

    Brains are taken out of the skull, labelled for pS129 and TH, and cleared with an optimised iDISCO+ protocol.

  5. Light-sheet imaging

    The whole cleared brain is imaged on a mesoSPIM in three channels at 2.75 × 2.75 × 4 µm.

  6. Registration

    PRISM maps the MRI and LSM volumes of each animal into DevCCF space on a common 20 µm grid.

MRI protocol

Bruker BioSpec 94/30 with a cryogenic 2 × 2 phased-array surface coil. Values as reported in the source study.

Registration with PRISM

PRISM (Pipeline for Registration and Integration of Structural and Molecular Imaging) runs two coarse-to-fine workflows in parallel. The T1-weighted volume is bias-corrected and registered to the DevCCF MRI template with rigid, affine and symmetric-normalisation (SyN) stages inside a brain mask. The autofluorescence volume is downsampled, registered to the DevCCF LSM template, and then refined against the template-aligned T1-weighted MRI with a cross-modal SyN step. The resulting transforms are applied to the pS129 and TH channels.

Accuracy was checked on seven anatomical landmarks, in ventricles and major white-matter tracts, across three iDISCO+-processed brains: the mean MRI–LSM landmark error is 41.9 ± 22.4 µm.

05

Tasks

Three learning problems on the paired data.

Task 1 · Classification

Baseline reported

Pathological phenotype identification

PFF injection produces pathology that depends on brain region and on time. The task asks whether one LSM channel from one animal is enough to tell PFF from monomer injection, and which regions carry the evidence. One classifier is trained for every pair of channel and region.

Input
One LSM channel, pS129 or TH, cropped to one atlas-defined region with a two-voxel margin
Target
Injection condition: PFF or monomer
Regions
SNc, VTA, CP, BST, CEA and PPN, each split into the injected (ipsilateral) and the opposite (contralateral) hemisphere: 12 regions
Grid
13.75 × 13.75 × 20 µm, with Allen CCFv3 labels mapped into each animal's own space
Animals
TH: 37 (18 PFF, 19 monomer). pS129: 26 (18 PFF, 8 monomer)
Protocol
Five-fold cross-validation at animal level, stratified by group and time point, repeated with three fold partitions
Metric
AUC from the pooled out-of-fold predictions
Baseline
3D ResNet-18, trained from scratch for 60 epochs

Task 2 · Cross-channel synthesis

Baselines reported

TH synthesis from α-synuclein pathology

Whole-brain immunolabelling is limited by the number of fluorescence channels. Predicting TH from channels that are already acquired would free one for another marker, and would allow dopaminergic integrity to be assessed in animals without TH imaging.

pS129 alone does not determine a TH image, so each animal is also given a prior: the mean TH image of the monomer controls, mapped into its own space. A model predicts the departure from that prior.

T^i = Bi + Rθ (Pi,Bi)

Prior B

Output
The TH volume of that animal, on its own grid

Task 3 · Guided super-resolution

Protocol in preparation

LSM-guided MRI super-resolution

High-field MRI of a mouse brain at fine resolution takes many hours of scanning. The autofluorescence channel shows tissue structure at micrometre sampling without any label, and it is registered to the MRI of the same animal. The task is to use it as an anatomical guide for recovering fine detail from low-resolution MRI.

Input
Low-resolution MRI and the registered LSM autofluorescence volume of the same animal
Target
The MRI volume at full resolution
Reference grid
MRI resampled to 50 µm isotropic in DevCCF-aligned LSM space
What makes it hard
Autofluorescence and MRI measure different tissue properties; clearing deforms tissue non-linearly; registration leaves a residual error
Status
The evaluation protocol and baseline results will be published with the paper
